  • The shishi-odoshi: a green bamboo tube pivoting between two short posts, its open end raised under a thin spout from a tall green kakei post on the right, its other end resting over a dark stone. Water trickles from the spout into the tube.
  • Stones: two large pale, speckled boulders at the back left; moss-capped rounded stones ringing the pond; flat, wet, dark stepping stones set in the moss on the left.
  • The lantern: a tall weathered granite lantern at the back left, with a broad roof, fire box, middle platform and banded column, moss on its upper faces.
  • The pond: a dark, still, sunken pond in the foreground, reflecting the stones, with red and pale leaves floating on it.
  • Planting: a bright green moss carpet; large ferns behind the tube and at the right; clumps of tall, jointed tokusa (scouring rush) against the fence; sparse grass tufts.

How it was read

  • Standalone HTML: one file that runs from disk. No build tools, network requests, libraries, models, images or audio files are loaded at run time. The only embedded asset is Clearwater's own pebble texture (base64), used for the pond bed. Every mesh, the foliage texture and every sound is generated when the page loads.
  • Interactive: the shishi-odoshi is a physical simulation, not an animation loop. It fills, tips, pours, swings back and strikes the stone with a synthesised “kon” and its echo. The flow, rain, wind and time of day are adjustable. You can tap the bamboo to tip it, tap the pond to raise ripples, and drop maple leaves onto the water.
  • Use Clearwater: Clearwater draws open water, not scenes. Its water code (FFT spectrum, ripple simulation, caustics, glints, pebble bed, glare and tone mapping) is kept and rewired as the water layer of a rasterised garden. The Implementation tab lists what was kept and what changed.

Architecture

Clearwater is a single-file WebGL2 program. Each pixel of one full-screen shader intersects an infinite water plane, refracts down to a pebble bed, and adds caustics, Fresnel reflection of an analytic sky, and sun glints. It has no geometry and no depth buffer. This build adds a rasteriser around it. The garden is drawn first into an HDR target with a depth texture, including the pond's walls and bed below the water line. Clearwater's water shader then runs as a compositing pass: wherever the pond surface is nearer than the garden, it shades that surface. Refraction comes from the garden image and reflection from a mirrored render. Transparent layers (water jets, droplets, splash crowns and rain) are drawn last against the garden's depth.

One frame

  1. Shishi-odoshi physicsFixed 240 Hz steps of the tube's rigid-body equation (below). The kakei jet is traced as a ballistic stream each frame; where it lands decides whether the tube fills.
  2. Wave spectrum → FFTClearwater's Tessendorf-style spectrum on a shorter 3.2 m patch, scaled down to the faint wind ripple of a sheltered pond (tied to the Wind slider).
  3. Ripple simulationClearwater's wave-equation grid, fitted to the pond (512 × 800 texels, about 5 mm each) and stepped at 120 Hz. A bank mask pins the height to zero on land and under stones, so rings reflect off the rim. Up to 48 disturbances per step come from raindrops, the kakei jet, the pour, falling leaves and taps.
  4. Caustics and sun shadowClearwater's refracted-grid caustics and a 2048² shadow map. Both run only when the rain eases enough for the sun to break through.
  5. Reflection passThe garden rendered through a mirror at the water line at half resolution, clipped below it.
  6. Garden passSky, moss, stones, fence, lantern, the tube (with its own model matrix), ferns, tokusa, grass, the maple and the bamboo grove beyond the fence, and floating leaves bobbing on the ripple field.
  7. Water compositeClearwater's water shader: height-field intersection, B-spline-filtered slopes, Fresnel, absorption and in-scattering, LEAN-widened glints and the suspended specks. Added: procedural raindrop rings with splash specks on top of the simulated ones, and ground mist.
  8. Transparent passThe kakei trickle and the tube's pour as camera-facing ribbons shaded like round jets. Flying droplets, splash crowns on stone and moss, and up to 9,000 rain streaks, all generated in the vertex shader.
  9. PostClearwater's bloom, FFT lens-diffraction glare (only with sun), ACES tone curve and grain. Added: depth of field for the close viewpoints.

The shishi-odoshi

The tube is one rigid body with one degree of freedom, its angle θ about the axle (positive means mouth up). It is a 12 cm culm 1.2 m long. The empty tube's centre of mass sits 8.5 cm behind the axle, so it rests with its closed end on the stone at θ = 20°. The water collects against the chamber's node. Its lever arm grows as the tube tilts, because the water slides toward the mouth; that is what makes the tip run away once it starts.

I(V) θ̈ = m g c cos θ − ρV g a(V, θ) cos θ − k θ̇ I(V) = I₀ + ρV a², m = 1.3 kg, c = 8.5 cm, I₀ = 0.085 kg m² a(V, θ) = node + ½ min(Lc, V/A + ½ r cot θ) (water's lever arm) V_max(θ) = A · clamp(Lc − r cot θ, 0, Lc) (water spills over the lowest point of the lip) strike at θ = 20°: θ̇ → −0.34 θ̇, a "kon" whose loudness follows the impact speed

At the default flow of 4.8 L/min the tube tips after about 0.8 L, roughly every 11 seconds. It dips to about −24°, pours in half a second and strikes the stone at about 1.65 m/s. A second, softer knock follows 0.24 s later as it bounces, the familiar “ko-kon”. More flow gives a shorter cycle and a deeper dip. The striking stone was generated first, then raised or lowered until its crown touches the tube's closed end at exactly the rest angle.

Sound

Everything is synthesised with Web Audio when you turn the sound on. The kon is modal synthesis: six damped sine modes of a hollow culm (strongest near 740 Hz, with overtones and a low air-column mode) plus a band-passed click for the stone. It goes through a short garden reverb and a 140 ms echo off the house. The trickle is a loop of tiny bubble sounds; its playback rate rises as the tube fills, like pouring water into a bottle, and it switches to a plashing sound when the jet falls into the pond instead. The pour is a denser, lower bubble loop. The rain is pink noise plus thousands of tiny drop clicks, with its level and brightness following the rain slider. Sounds are panned by where the shishi-odoshi sits relative to the camera.

What was written for the garden

  • Layout: positions come from the reference frames. The pond outline is a Catmull-Rom curve with a cached signed-distance field. The ground is a 5 cm grid of moss over gentle mounds, dished into a steep-sided pond 35–45 cm deep.
  • Sky occlusion under overcast light: the yard is rendered once from straight above into a 1024² height field. The moss, ferns, tokusa and grass measure the horizon in eight directions from it, which gives the soft contact darkening you see around stones, posts and the fence foot. The rain uses the same map to stop at the first surface, so no rain falls under the lantern roof or through the pond. Splash crowns are placed on it too.
  • Moss: a shader built from four noise scales for cushions, tufts and stems, with relief applied by surface-gradient bump mapping. It covers the ground, caps the rounded stones and fringes the stepping stones.
  • Stones: icospheres cut by random planes, blended with smooth-min so river-worn stones stay rounded, and roughened with noise. The pale boulders carry mica specks, lichen crust and speckled moss.
  • Bamboo: the fence is nearly 800 individual culms with random diameters and node spacing, backed by a second row so no light shows through, plus posts, four rails per side and rope ties. The shader draws nodes as ridges, a waxy bloom under each node on fresh bamboo, striations, weathering, lichen, a damp foot, and beads of rain on green bamboo seen close.
  • Plants: fern fronds are folded ribbons textured with fronds painted in Canvas 2D at load (once- and twice-divided kinds). Tokusa stems are jointed tubes with black sheaths and pale rims. There are grass tufts, a maple in the front corner whose leaves fall into the pond and drift there, and a madake grove beyond the fence.
  • Rain: streaks are built in the vertex shader from the vertex ID alone and wrapped in a box that follows the camera. The pond shows rings from both the ripple simulation and a procedural layer. Stone and bamboo get splash crowns, and surfaces darken and turn glossy with wetness, then dry slowly after the rain stops.

This session's numbers

Limits and assumptions

  • The layout, proportions and materials follow the reference frames as closely as could be judged by eye. The garden camera was fitted numerically to landmark positions measured on the reference frame. Sizes that the frames do not show directly were set from typical practice: a 12 cm tube about 1.2 m long, a fence about 2.35 m tall and a lantern about 1.75 m tall.
  • The reference tube appears open at both ends in close-up; this one has the usual closed node at the striking end, since that end is what makes the sound.
  • Fern fronds and leaves are alpha-tested textures on folded ribbons and cards, so they are flatter than real foliage up close. Moss is a surface shader, not geometry.
  • Refraction is screen-space and only adds the bending caused by waves. Reflections come from a single mirror plane. The water inside the tube is simulated but not drawn.
  • The page needs WebGL2 with floating-point render targets. Resolution adapts to hold the frame rate. Sound starts only after you press Turn on sound, as browsers require.

Elements of the garden

A small enclosed courtyard garden (tsubo-niwa scale) of the kind seen from a veranda: moss, stones, a lantern, a pond, and the knock of the shishi-odoshi, with the fence shutting out the world.

鹿威しShishi-odoshi“Deer-scarer”. A pivoting bamboo tube that fills, tips and knocks against a stone. Farmers used it to startle animals from their fields; in gardens, the regular knock marks the passing of time in a quiet place.
筧KakeiThe bamboo pipe that brings water. Here it is a tall culm with a thin spout let into its side.
添水SōzuAnother name for the device, used especially in Kyoto, as at Shisen-dō, where one has knocked since the 17th century.
竹垣TakegakiThe bamboo fence: close-set round culms, pale rails (oshibuchi), posts, and black shuro-palm rope ties.
石灯籠Ishi-dōrōThe stone lantern: base (kiso), column (sao), middle platform (chūdai), fire box (hibukuro), roof (kasa) and jewel (hōju). It lights at dusk, or when you tap it.
苔KokeMoss. It thrives in the humid shade of Kyoto gardens and is prized for its deep, soft green, never brighter than after rain.
飛石Tobi-ishiStepping stones, set a stride apart so the walker's pace slows and the eye moves along the path.
木賊TokusaScouring rush (Equisetum hyemale), whose upright jointed stems are a classic fence-side planting.
羊歯ShidaFerns, soft against the hard stones and bamboo.
紅葉MomijiA Japanese maple in the front corner, just out of the garden view. Its leaves fall and drift on the pond.
池IkeThe pond: sunken, stone-rimmed, dark with tannin from fallen leaves, so it works mostly as a mirror.
雨AmeRain. The garden is meant to be seen wet: stones darken, moss glows, bamboo shines.

This build

The garden generation, the rendering around Clearwater, the shishi-odoshi physics, the rain, the sound synthesis and the interface were written for this page. It reuses parts of an earlier Clearwater garden build from the same workspace (Tsukiyama Garden): the mesh helpers, the stone generator and the compositing approach. Techniques used: surface-gradient bump mapping (Mikkelsen), horizon-based sky occlusion from a height field, a Kasten–Young air mass for sun colour, Henyey–Greenstein in-scattering (as in Clearwater), planar reflections with slope distortion, and modal synthesis for the knock.
