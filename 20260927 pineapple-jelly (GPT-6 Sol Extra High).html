Implementation details How the specimen works
Browser & rendering
One standalone file contains both renderers. Automatic mode prefers genuine WebGPU with inline WGSL shaders and falls back to CPU software 3D when the API, adapter, shader initialization, or graphics device is unavailable. The Rendering selector also lets you choose either mode. Renderer changes preserve the simulation. No WebGL, external libraries, models, image assets, or server are required.
Volumetric soft body
The XPBD (Extended Position-Based Dynamics) simulation uses 384 nodes and 1,152 tetrahedra. Fixed 1/120-second steps run six iterations of elastic edges, bending connections, and signed tetrahedral volume constraints. A final volume barrier resists locally inverted cells. Gravity, ground contact, friction, air drag, and internal damping create impact squashing and a softly settling wobble.
Separate visual surface
A smoother 5,184-vertex annular surface has broad cut faces, rounded corners, a slightly scalloped outer edge, and a real central opening. Cubic angular and linear radial/depth interpolation transfer displacement from the coarser simulation mesh. Surface normals are recalculated after deformation, and the mesh toggle draws the simulation connections.
Flesh & inclusions
Procedural radial fibers and cut-face patterns use interpolated rest-space coordinates, so they follow the deforming flesh on both sides. Another 330 embedded 3D ellipsoids form pale radial juice cells and small air bubbles. WebGPU skins and draws their complete meshes. CPU mode skins six axis endpoints to determine each ellipsoid’s projected shape and depth-tests its pixels inside the jelly.
Refraction & light
Both paths measure local optical thickness from front and back depths, using Snell refraction, Beer–Lambert absorption, Fresnel reflection, wet highlights, and wrapped light scattering. WebGPU performs the material shading in WGSL with multisample antialiasing. CPU mode rasterizes actual 3D triangles into software depth buffers with perspective-correct attributes and presents the generated pixels through Canvas 2D. Code-generated lookup textures accelerate its radial fibers, absorption, and highlights. Both paths use screen-space studio refraction and approximate scattering and soft contact shadows.
Grabbing & viewing
Ray–triangle picking selects actual flesh, including the inner wall. A ray through the unobstructed hole selects nothing. A compliant weighted attachment spreads the pull through nearby simulation nodes. Mouse and touch use pointer capture. Scroll during a grab to pull in depth; drag empty space or use two fingers to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom.
Controls & readouts
Golden, Amber, and Rosé change absorption tint with the same material model. Firmness changes elastic compliance; Internal damping controls relative-velocity dissipation. Nudge adds velocity impulses. Turn it over uses temporary soft targets to lift and rotate the ring before release. Reset restores the rest geometry; Pause freezes simulation; ¼ speed slows time. Readouts show the assigned 164 g mass, relative signed volume, kinetic energy, and render rate.
Validation
Automatic fallback and CPU mode are checked in Chromium with WebGPU unavailable and WebGL disabled, using direct file loading. Controlled adapter rejection, shader initialization failure, device loss, and renderer changes check recovery and preservation of simulation state. Checks cover outer- and inner-edge pulls, depth pulling, the open central hole, soft recovery, flipping, reset, pause, color presets, and mesh display. Touch dragging, release, and pinch/orbit controls are checked with mobile emulation. Native WebGPU rendering cannot be retested on this VM because it provides no adapter. Software resolution adapts to frame cost, and rendering idles while paused or while the scene is offscreen.
Original WebGPU prompt The complete brief that inspired this specimen

CPU edition request: “Please provide CPU edition. I do not have WebGPU in VM.”

Compatibility request: “Is it possible the same web page can support both WebGPU and non-WebGPU environments?”

Create an interactive 3D scene called “Pineapple Jelly” - a thick, translucent gummy pineapple ring that users can grab, stretch, bend, and release. Everything must run directly in the browser in one standalone HTML file using genuine WebGPU and WGSL shaders.
The main priorities are convincing soft-body behavior, beautiful light refraction, and satisfying interaction.

MODEL

  • A round pineapple slice with a genuine hole through its center.
  • Thick, juicy flesh, a slightly scalloped outer edge, and softly rounded corners.
  • Both cut faces must be equally detailed. The ring can be flipped and viewed from every angle.
  • Golden translucent flesh with delicate radial fibers, small bubbles, and elongated pale juice cells embedded inside.
  • No skin, leaves, or seeds. It should look like a pineapple ring made of gummy candy, not a doughnut.

MATERIAL AND LIGHTING

  • Rich honey-yellow coloring, wet gloss, and soft subsurface-style light scattering.
  • Refraction, thickness-dependent transparency, controlled highlights, and Fresnel reflections around the edges.
  • Stretched, thinner areas should transmit more light.
  • A warm, light background, soft studio lighting, and a grounded contact shadow.
  • Avoid blown-out highlights, excessive bloom, and a hard-glass appearance.

PHYSICS

  • Use a volumetric soft-body mesh with elastic connections and volume constraints, such as XPBD.
  • Allow users to grab either the outer or inner edge and pull it in any direction.
  • Deformation should spread naturally through neighboring regions: the hole stretches, the flesh bends, and the opposite side responds with a slight delay.
  • After release, the ring should spring back softly, wobble a few times, and gradually settle.
  • No instant snapping, endless oscillation, torn geometry, or collapsing central hole.
  • Implement gravity and ground collisions. The ring should squash slightly on impact and recover its shape.
  • Internal fibers, bubbles, and juice cells must deform with the flesh instead of remaining stationary.
  • Clicking through the empty center must not grab the object.

INTERFACE

  • A minimal editorial layout with generous whitespace.
  • A large italic serif heading on the left: “Pineapple Jelly.”
  • A compact “THE SPECIMEN” control panel on the right.
  • Three color presets: Golden, Amber, and Rosé. Change the tint while preserving the material properties.
  • Sliders for Firmness and Internal damping.
  • Buttons: Give it a nudge, Reset, Turn it over, and Pause.
  • Toggles: ¼ speed and Show mesh.
  • Small live readouts for mass, volume relative to rest volume, and kinetic energy.
  • All interface text in English.
  • Support both mouse and touch interaction.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

  • Use actual 3D geometry and WebGPU rendering, not a video or a Canvas 2D imitation.
  • Generate all geometry and materials in code, without external models or image assets.
  • Separate the simulation mesh from the smoother visual surface to maintain good performance.
  • Display a clear fallback message when WebGPU is unavailable.
  • Test strong stretching, inner-edge grabbing, flipping, resetting, and mobile interaction.

Save the finished result as pineapple-jelly-webgpu.html. Deliver the complete working file, not a mockup or implementation plan.
The final experience should feel like a soft, stretchy, appetizing gummy toy that is genuinely fun to squish.
