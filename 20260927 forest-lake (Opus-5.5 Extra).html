How this landscape was made

The request, and how the page turns it into a 3D world. Everything is generated in code when the page opens.

The prompt

“create landscape in 3D interactive view, hosted in standalone html: A forest with a lake in the center. In the middle of the lake, an abandoned house. Around the lake, a flower-filled French village. Paths connecting the important points.”

Implementation

One file, no assetsThe page has no 3D models, image textures or data files. The only download is three.js r160 (with OrbitControls and BufferGeometryUtils) from the jsDelivr CDN, loaded through an import map. A seeded random generator (mulberry32, seed 1789) makes the world the same on every load.
Layout measured from the shorelineThe lake's outline is a sum of sines, r(θ) = 70 + 6·sin(3θ+.7) + 3.5·sin(5θ+2.1) + 2·sin(8θ+.3) metres. Everything else is placed by an angle and a distance from that shoreline: the promenade at +12 m, the Grande Rue at +32 m, rows of houses at +21 m and +44 m, forest that thickens between +46 m and +80 m, and the chapel at +118 m.
TerrainA 1 km × 1 km grid with 400 × 400 segments (160,801 vertices). Height = a lake basin down to −8 m + a ramp up from the shore + gentle noise across the village + multi-octave value-noise hills that rise outward into a wooded bowl. Circular pads flatten the ground under the square, church, windmill and chapel. Colour is stored per vertex: lake bed, sand, grass, forest floor, path dirt and lavender soil.
PathsNine Catmull-Rom splines are draped over the terrain as ribbon meshes with slightly ragged edges. Their sample points go into an 8 m spatial hash, so any point can quickly find its distance to the nearest path. That keeps houses, trees and flowers off the paths and tints the grass beside them.
BuildingsA small kit places boxes, cylinders and a gable-roof prism in a building's local frame. Each house picks its own size, number of storeys, wall, roof and shutter colours, window boxes, balcony, climbing plants, hedge and garden tree, then goes wherever a free spot exists. All the buildings (village, church, windmill, chapel, abandoned house and boardwalk) are merged into one geometry per material, which makes five draw calls. The windmill sails and the rowboat stay separate so they can move. Windows, lamps and the haunted window each get their own material so they can glow at night.
VegetationTrees (pine, oak, poplar/cypress, blossom, willow), flowers, shrubs, lavender, sunflowers, reeds, lily pads and rocks are drawn as InstancedMeshes with a colour per instance. A patch to the vertex shader (onBeforeCompile) makes the trees sway.
WaterMeshPhysicalMaterial with two tileable normal maps, generated in code and scrolling in different directions (a base layer and a clear-coat layer). The reflections come from a PMREM environment map rendered from the sky shader. It is re-rendered whenever the time of day changes.
Light and time of dayA gradient-sky shader draws the sun or moon disc. A hemisphere light fills the shadows, and a directional light casts soft shadows (4096² map, or 2048² on smaller GPUs) over a 430 m square, all with ACES filmic tone mapping. The three presets blend into each other over about 2 seconds. The amount of "night" controls the glowing windows and lamps, the pools of light under the lamps, the stars, 420 fireflies, the mist, and a flickering light inside the abandoned house.
InteractionOrbit uses OrbitControls with damping and keeps the camera above the ground. Clicking a place flies the camera there along an eased arc, and the guided tour orbits slowly at each stop. The labels are HTML, positioned from 3D coordinates every frame. Walk mode is first-person at 1.7 m eye height and follows the ground. It starts on the path nearest to where you were looking, far enough back to see the buildings there whole, facing that spot, with a wider 68° field of view. It rides on the boardwalk deck, and stops you at the water, the fountain and each building's actual footprint.
