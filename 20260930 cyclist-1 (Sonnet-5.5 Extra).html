notion-style graphics in standalone web page: cyclist riding a bicycle.

Source URL

None. The prompt was typed directly; there is no reference video, post or image. Everything on this page is original and procedural — no libraries, fonts, bitmaps or network requests.

What “Notion style” means here

  • Ink lines. Thick, round-capped, near-black outlines that are never perfectly straight.
  • Flat pastel blocks. Colour is a plain fill drawn offset from the outline by (3, 3.6) px, like a slightly misregistered print. Solid black shapes (hair, shorts, saddle) sit exactly inside their outline.
  • Sparse doodles on warm paper: lollipop trees, scribble clouds, a hot-air balloon, sparkles and hearts — and a matching dark mode.

How it is built

  • Hand-drawn strokes. Every shape is resampled and pushed sideways by a few low-frequency sines: a fixed “wonkiness” per shape plus a line boil re-rolled 9 times a second, the way hand-drawn animation shimmers. Paths are smoothed with quadratic curves.
  • Rider and bike. Side view, all in one local coordinate system. Legs and arms are two-bone IK (hip→pedal, shoulder→hood); the cranks turn at the same rate as the wheel divided by the gear ratio (chainring r 26 : cog r 15 ≈ 1.75), chain dashes flow, and the ponytail is a 7-node Verlet chain blown back by the speed.
  • Endless scenery. Seven parallax layers (sky 0.06, far hills 0.10, hills 0.22, mid doodles 0.45, near doodles 0.8, road 1.0, foreground flowers 1.4). Objects are placed by hashing cell numbers, so the road never repeats and nothing is stored.
  • Performance. One transparent Canvas 2D layer, no filters or shadows, off-screen objects skipped, device-pixel-ratio capped at 2. Everything is drawn in design units and scaled to the window.
  • Palette. Two token sets (paper, ink, yellow, blue, pink, coral, green, lavender, skin) — swap them in PALS.
