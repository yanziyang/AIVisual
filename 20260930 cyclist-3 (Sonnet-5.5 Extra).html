How it is built

  • Standalone. One HTML file. three.js r186 is inlined (tree-shaken to the classes used, MIT licence), so it runs offline. There are no images, fonts or models: every mesh and texture is generated in code.
  • Bike. Real road-bike numbers: 73° seat / 72.5° head angle, 405 mm chainstays, 700c wheels, 172.5 mm cranks, 46T ring, 11–32 cassette, disc brakes. The frame is merged tubes with a clear-coat paint; the fork blades are tapered lofts.
  • Live drivetrain. The chain is a closed loop of instanced links wrapped by tangent lines around ring, cog and both derailleur pulleys. The cage angle is solved every frame so the loop stays taut, and the rider auto-shifts to keep cadence at 70–100 rpm; you can see the chain step across the cassette.
  • Rider. Lofted torso, lathe-profile limbs, helmet with conforming vents, mirrored glasses, shoes and gloves. Two-bone IK puts each foot on its pedal and each hand on its hood; the saddle height and torso angle are fitted to the leg and arm lengths at start-up. Standing lifts the pelvis, rocks the bike and sways the hips so the centre of mass stays over the wheels.
  • Wheels. 28 tangentially laced spokes. Above a wheel speed where spokes would strobe against the frame rate, a soft motion-blur disc cross-fades in and the spokes fade out.
  • World. An endless winding road (heading is a sum of sines, position integrated), streamed in 48 m chunks. Terrain is built in road-aligned coordinates with a ditch and verge, fields on a world grid, forests from fractal noise, instanced trees / bushes / grass / flowers with a wind-sway shader, fences, barns, power lines, and three haze-blended mountain rings.
  • Sky & light. A shader dome (gradient, sun disc and glow, stars, Milky-Way band, moon) driven by time of day and by a weather blend (clear, hazy, overcast, rain: fog, palette, sun strength, cloud cover, wet road, rain streaks); the reflection environment is re-baked with PMREM as the sun moves. Sun shadows follow the bike with texel snapping. At night the headlight (spot plus a soft beam cone) and tail light take over.
  • Camera. An auto-director cuts between seven shots (front ¾, side track, drivetrain close-up, fly-by, chase, low wheel, drone) with eased blends. Drag, pinch or scroll to take over; it resumes after a few seconds.
