How it works

  • Pressure. The gauge reads a Gent rubber model for a spherical membrane, P = P₀ (λ⁻¹ − λ⁻⁷) / (1 − (2λ² + λ⁻⁴ − 3)/Jm), where λ is the cube root of the volume ratio. Each stroke pushes a fixed volume while the handle travels. Jm, P₀ and the burst pressure are drawn per round from a seeded generator, so the limit is hidden. The pressure peaks during the first stroke, falls, then climbs steeply near lock-up.
  • Shape. The skin is a 128 × 65 grid in the rubber's own coordinates (meridian u × azimuth v), rebuilt on the CPU every frame. Uninflated, it is a collapsed elliptical tube that flops over. Inflation follows the two-phase instability: the bulb at the neck end takes a growing share of the material, and the slack tip drapes over it until only the thick nub is left. Near the limit a Gaussian aneurysm grows at a seeded weak spot. The neck is a Bézier that bends towards the swung axis, so the nozzle stays rigid.
  • Stretch and thickness. Area stretch is measured from the deformed grid against the rest metric, and thickness = rest thickness ÷ area stretch. The print uses the same rest coordinates, so it starts tiny and crumpled. Beyond about 4× local stretch the ink, which stretches less than rubber, breaks into Voronoi islands; the @vib3coded line is a separate layer and never cracks.
  • Latex rendering. The scene renders into a linear half-float target. Each wall is drawn twice, back wall first. The multiply pass applies per-channel Beer–Lambert transmittance exp(−α·h·path) from the dye, with a longer path at grazing angles. The additive pass adds Kubelka–Munk-style scattering, a GGX clearcoat against an analytic softbox studio, and a backlit translucency term. The result is a final Khronos PBR Neutral tone map.
  • Shadow. The floor shadow is ray-cast per pixel against an ellipsoid fitted to the bulb, plus one for the slack pouch. Transmittance through two walls tints the centre and darkens the rim. Water adds a ball-lens caustic.
  • Pop. The tear front runs from the weak spot round the skin in about 34 ms (two frames) with a ragged edge, and the rubber thickens along it. 18–28 shreds take their stretched shape from the skin at the instant the front reaches them, then contract to rest size (τ ≈ 12 ms). They fly with quadratic drag, flutter and land. A spring-driven frill stays on the neck, and a talc shell lingers briefly. Water bodies fall, split on the nozzle, crown, sheet and leave a puddle.
  • Replay. The pop runs on a fixed 1/960 s step from a seeded generator. The last half-second before the burst is recorded, so the replay at ×12 tears the same balloon the same way.
  • Sound. Everything is synthesised: noise-band piston whoosh, valve click, handle thunk, latex squeaks and stick-slip creaks, membrane modes for the flick (pitch ∝ √(Pλ)), crack plus sub thump, water slaps and bubble drips, all through a generated room impulse response.
  • Performance. Every shader, including the pop-only ones, is compiled and drawn once at start-up, and a hidden pop of each kind is simulated so the burst code is already optimised. Pixel ratio adapts to frame time, and prefers-reduced-motion removes camera easing, shake and idle drift.

Prompt

Build "The Balloon Test": a single-file, mobile-friendly 3D toy in one HTML page (Three.js bundled inline, Web Audio, no external assets except Google Fonts).

Scene: a warm paper studio. On the right, a latex balloon is pulled over the chrome nozzle of a weighted stand. On the left, a floor pump: charcoal barrel, chrome rod, wooden T-handle and a round pressure gauge (0–8 kPa, red zone from 5), joined to the stand by a rubber hose. The camera reframes as the balloon grows, keeps the pump in view and leaves room for the HUD. On tall phones the pump stands further back.

Play: tap anywhere (or Space) to pump. The handle drives down, air moves while it travels, the hose twitches and the gauge needle overshoots slightly and settles. The limit is hidden and random per round. Captions escalate ("The first breath is the hardest.", "Easier now. Watch the needle.", "Getting round.", "That is a lot of balloon.", "The rubber is getting thin.", "Please stop."). Flick (F) taps the skin: a ripple runs over it, and the note rises as it tightens. Water toggle (W), reset (R).

Physics you can read:
- Inflation pressure follows a Gent rubber model. It peaks on the first stroke, falls as the balloon grows, then climbs steeply near the limit, and the gauge shows exactly that.
- Inflation shape: the uninflated balloon is a flat, limp pouch that flops over. Early on, only the neck end inflates while the slack tip still lies on top. Then it becomes a teardrop with a small thick nub at the tip. Near the limit, a weak spot bulges out (an aneurysm).
- It swings about the nozzle (the neck bends, the nozzle does not), with a light idle drift. Water balloons are squatter, heavier and jiggle.

Rendering the latex (the core of the look):
- Draw each wall in two passes, back wall first, then front. A multiply pass filters the background by per-channel Beer–Lambert transmittance from the dye colour, with thickness = rest thickness / area stretch and longer paths at grazing angles. An additive pass adds scattered light, clearcoat reflections and a little translucency glow.
- The result: deep, saturated colour when slack, glassy and tinted when stretched, with a saturated rim and lighter centre. Slack rubber shows wrinkles. The neck has a rolled lip.
- A coloured floor shadow from an analytic ellipsoid: tinted in the middle, dark at the rim. Water balloons add a caustic.
- A printed design glued to the rubber's own coordinates (star, polka dots, smiley, "POP? / HANDLE WITH CARE", hearts or plain). It starts tiny and crumpled, grows with the balloon, and cracks into a mosaic when overstretched. Every balloon also carries "@vib3coded" in the same ink, printed from a separate layer so it stays legible while the motif cracks.
- Water mode: a slightly smaller inner skin with real transmission/refraction.

The pop, in real time (no pause):
- It goes on the final stroke. A ragged tear front runs from the weak spot round the skin in a couple of frames, the rubber bunching along its edge.
- Rubber snaps back to its unstretched size, so the debris is 18–28 small, thick, deeply coloured shreds (crumpled patches and curled ribbons). Each leaves from where it was on the skin at the moment the tear reaches it: stretched at first, contracting in flight, flung out fast, braking hard with quadratic air drag, then fluttering and tumbling down onto the floor.
- A ragged frill of rubber strips stays on the nozzle: springy, curling outwards, settling quickly. A brief talc puff shows the balloon's shape.
- Water: spray is flung from where the rubber snaps. The rest falls at once, splits on the nozzle, hits the stand and the floor in a crown of drops, spreads as a thin sheet, then leaves a puddle and wet marks.
- All randomness comes from a seeded generator, so the end screen can offer "Replay in slow motion" (×12, key S), which tears the same balloon the same way.

End screen: "Pop." or "Splash.", then pumps · stretch at burst (e.g. 7.4×) · kPa, a verdict ("A cheap party balloon." / "Heavy-duty rubber."), a personal best kept in localStorage, and "Do it again".

Sound (procedural, with a small synthetic room reverb): piston whoosh, valve click and handle thunk; latex squeaks and creaks rising with pressure; a membrane "boing" for the flick; a sharp crack plus sub thump for the pop; slaps and drips for water; ticks as shreds land; a deep slowed boom for the replay.

Design: editorial, quiet. Small masthead top-left ("THE BALLOON TEST / How much air can it take?"), mono-caps controls top-right (Water, Flick, Reset, Sound), "Pumps N | x.x kPa" plus an italic caption bottom-centre, a "● Slow motion ×12" slate during the replay. Random balloon colour per round, with the UI accent following it.

Quality bar: smooth on phones, portrait and landscape, precompile every shader at startup so the pop never stalls, respects prefers-reduced-motion, no console errors.
