How it is made

  • One file, no libraries, no images. Everything is drawn each frame with the Canvas 2D API, sized to devicePixelRatio (capped at 2). Fonts are system fonts.
  • Infinite parallax scenery. The world is generated on the fly from a hash function, so nothing repeats or is stored: mountains and hills are sums of sine ridges; the skyline, trees and roadside props come from hashed grid cells. Eight scrolling layers (clouds 0.03×, mountains 0.06×, hills 0.14×, skyline 0.3×, trees 0.5×, road-side props 1×, lane paint 1.25×, foreground verge 1.7×) give the depth.
  • Day/night cycle. Four palette keyframes (dawn, day, sunset, night) are interpolated. Each layer’s colour is multiplied by the current ambient tint and fogged toward the horizon colour. Stars, lit windows, street lamps, the bike’s headlight and its blinking tail light fade in with the night factor.
  • Believable wheels. Wheels roll without slipping (angle = distance ÷ radius). At speed the spokes would alias, so each frame is drawn as several sub-frame samples at low alpha, which gives real motion blur. The chain is a dashed line whose offset follows the crank.
  • Pedalling rider. Legs use analytic two-bone inverse kinematics from hip to ankle, with the ankle riding a pedal that stays level and ankles slightly through the stroke. Arms use the same IK from shoulder to the brake hoods. The torso rocks with the stroke and drops lower as speed rises. Cadence is derived from speed.
  • Scarf. A procedural travelling wave; it hangs when slow and streams horizontally when fast. Speed streaks and rear-wheel dust puffs scale with speed.
  • Title sequence. Masked letter reveal in CSS. The bike rolls in from the left with the wheel spin matched to its speed relative to the road, then the title docks to the corner.
