Half the pedal force, half the distance.

With a 34-tooth rear gear, one pedal turn makes the wheel turn once instead of twice. The chain pulls farther from the rear axle, giving the wheel twice the torque for the same pedal push.

About this page

Prompt

Show me why a bigger rear gear makes a bike easier to pedal uphill, host the animation in a standalone HTML.

Implementation details

  • One self-contained HTML file uses inline SVG, CSS, and JavaScript. The chainring, rear sprocket, chain, and wheel animate together; the slider changes the right rear sprocket from 17 to 42 teeth.
  • Wheel turns per pedal turn = front teeth ÷ rear teeth. In the ideal model, wheel torque scales with rear teeth ÷ front teeth.
  • Uphill pedal force assumes a 34-tooth front ring, 80 kg rider and bike, 6% grade, 170 mm crank, and 340 mm wheel radius. Rolling resistance, air drag, drivetrain losses, and changes in force around the pedal stroke are omitted.
