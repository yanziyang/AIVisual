築山庭園Tsukiyama Garden: prompt and implementation

The prompt

Next, build interactive Tsukiyama (hill and pond garden) in standalone HTML. A Japanese-style landscape that features a pavilion, fish pond, plants, and rocks is generally known as a Chisen-shoyū-teien (pond garden) or a Tsukiyama (hill and pond garden). Use open source Clearwater engine: https://github.com/Aureliengmz/clearwater Show the prompt and implementation details in the html web page.

Given on 27 September 2026. A follow-up in the same session asked for all working materials (sources, the Clearwater clone, the layout check) to be kept in the project folder.

How it was read

  • Standalone HTML: one file that runs from disk. No build tools, network requests, libraries, models or image downloads at run time. The only embedded asset is Clearwater's own pebble texture (base64). Everything else is generated when the page loads.
  • A real tsukiyama, not a generic park: artificial hills (tsukiyama) behind a pond with islands, a waterfall arrangement, a pebble beach, stepping stones, stone lanterns, a pavilion over the water, an arched bridge, pruned pines, maples, cherries, clipped azaleas, irises, water lilies and koi. The Garden tab lists each element and where it sits.
  • Use Clearwater: Clearwater renders open water, not scenes. So its water code (FFT spectrum, ripple simulation, caustics, glints, pebble bed, glare and tone mapping) is kept and rewired as the water layer of a rasterised garden. The Implementation tab lists exactly what was kept and what changed.
  • Interactive: orbit, a stroll mode, preset viewpoints, time of day, the four seasons, breeze, water clarity, rain, and feeding the koi. Tapping the water raises ripples, which reflect off the banks.

Architecture

Clearwater is a single-file WebGL2 program. Each pixel of one full-screen shader intersects an infinite water plane, refracts down to a pebble seabed, and adds caustics, Fresnel reflection of an analytic sky, and sun glints. It has no geometry, no depth buffer and no scene. This build adds a conventional rasteriser around it. The garden is drawn first, above and below the water, into an HDR target with a depth texture. Clearwater's water shader then runs as a compositing pass: wherever the water surface is nearer than the garden, it shades the surface using the garden image for refraction and a mirrored render for reflection.

One frame

  1. Wave spectrum → FFTClearwater's Tessendorf-style spectrum evaluated for the current time. An amplitude uniform turns open-sea chop into a garden-pond breeze tied to the Breeze slider.
  2. Ripple simulationClearwater's wave-equation grid, stretched from a 7 m window to the whole pond (1024×640 texels, about 4.7 cm each) and run at a fixed 60 Hz. A bank mask pins the height to zero on land, so rings bounce off the shore and islands. Up to 32 disturbances per step come from taps, koi wakes, feeding, raindrops, falling leaves and the waterfall.
  3. CausticsClearwater's refracted-grid caustics, unchanged. Three wavelengths of refraction give the rainbow fringes.
  4. Sun shadow mapA 4096² orthographic depth map of the terrain, buildings and alpha-tested leaves.
  5. Reflection passThe garden rendered through a mirror at y = 0 at half resolution, clipped at the waterline, with the sky and hazy distance.
  6. Garden passSky, terrain, rocks, pavilion, bridge, lanterns, branches, leaf cards, koi and particles. Anything below the water is lit by sunlight refracted at the surface, attenuated along its path, modulated by the caustics, and shadowed both by objects above the surface and by passing koi.
  7. Water compositeClearwater's water shader. It intersects the height field (FFT + ripples), reads refraction from the garden image displaced by the wave slope, and absorbs and scatters light along the underwater path. It adds Fresnel reflection from the mirror pass, sun glints and Clearwater's suspended specks, then applies aerial perspective to everything.
  8. WaterfallAn animated, alpha-blended sheet and plunge foam, depth-tested against the garden.
  9. PostClearwater's bloom, FFT lens-diffraction glare on sun glints, ACES tone curve and grain, plus a rain-streak overlay.

What was written for the garden

  • Layout and terrain: the pond outline is a Catmull-Rom curve. Heights come from a signed-distance field to the water's edge (rock-lined banks versus a gently shelving beach) plus Gaussian hills with noise, islands, a waterfall cliff and a levelled pad for the pavilion. It is meshed as a 0.25 m inner grid and a coarse outer ring.
  • Rocks: icospheres cut by random planes (smooth-min) and roughened with noise. There are shore rocks along every bank, and named groups: the waterfall stones, the carp stone, a triad, crane and turtle rocks, and stepping stones.
  • Pavilion: posts on stone footings, some standing in the pond; a floor frame, planks and tatami; shoji and plaster walls; railings. The hip-and-gable roof is built from a height function with a concave (sori) profile, lifted corners, tile relief, fascia, gable, barge boards and ridges.
  • Bridge and lanterns: an arched taiko-bashi with lacquered railings and bronze finials; kasuga, yukimi and two-legged kotoji lanterns whose fire boxes light up at dusk.
  • Plants: recursive branch tubes, plus leaf cards textured from a foliage atlas painted with Canvas 2D at load. The atlas channels hold shade, per-leaf variation and a flower flag, so a single texture yields spring, summer, autumn and winter colours, blossom and bare twigs. The pines are pruned into cloud pads with dark cores. Background cedars and evergreen oaks enclose the garden.
  • Koi: a lofted body with forked tail, dorsal, pectoral and pelvic fins. Swimming is a travelling wave in the vertex shader. There are eight varieties (Kohaku, Sanke, Shōwa, Yamabuki Ogon, Chagoi, Tanchō, Asagi, Platinum), each a procedural pattern. The fish wander, avoid the banks and each other, rise to food, dart away from taps, and cast soft shadows on the bed.
  • Seasons, weather and time: season weights blend smoothly. They switch foliage palettes, blossom, bare branches, flowering azaleas, irises and lilies, snow on up-facing surfaces, falling leaves, petals and snow. Sun colour uses an air-mass extinction model. Lanterns and shoji glow as the sun sets. Rain darkens and wets surfaces, overcasts the sky, and pocks the pond with rings.

Limits and assumptions

  • The garden is a composite of classic conventions, not a replica of a specific garden. No reference photographs were used. Proportions follow common practice (for example about 0.85 m floor height for the pavilion, 1.8 m bridge width, 1.9 m kasuga lantern).
  • Leaves are alpha-tested cards, so the canopies read well from a few metres away; close up they are flatter than real foliage. There is no grass-blade geometry.
  • Refraction is screen-space and only adds the bending caused by waves. The flat-water bending that makes a real pond look shallower is left out, because re-projecting it duplicated the koi. Things hidden behind the bank cannot appear through the water. The camera stays above the water, so there is no underwater view.
  • Reflections come from one mirror plane (the pond is flat), distorted by the surface slope. Glints and caustics come from the sun only.
  • The page needs WebGL2 with floating-point render targets. Resolution adapts to hold the frame rate; a discrete GPU is recommended for full resolution.

Elements of the garden

A chisen-shoyū-teien is a pond garden meant to be walked around, with views that open and close along the path. This one is laid out as the Edo-period stroll gardens were, with a Heian-style fishing pavilion. The gravel path circles the pond about 4.6 m back from the water.

築山TsukiyamaArtificial hills. The main one rises about 7.5 m behind the pavilion, flanked by lower hills to the north-east and north-west.
池IkeThe pond: about 45 × 26 m and up to about a metre deep, lined with rocks except at the beach and the iris margin.
中島NakajimaThe main island, reached by the arched bridge, with a spreading black pine and a small yukimi lantern.
太鼓橋Taiko-bashi“Drum bridge”: a steeply arched bridge with vermilion railings and bronze giboshi finials.
釣殿Tsuri-donoA fishing pavilion half over the water, on posts, with an irimoya (hip-and-gable) tiled roof, shoji and a tatami room.
滝石組Taki-ishigumiThe waterfall arrangement: a lip stone (mizuochi-ishi), two flanking stones (waki-ishi) and the carp stone (rigyoseki). The carp stone recalls the legend of the carp that became a dragon by climbing a waterfall.
亀島・鶴島Kame-jima & tsuru-jimaTurtle island and crane rocks, both symbols of longevity. The turtle's head stone points out into the pond; the crane's “wing” stone stands in the water.
州浜SuhamaA pebble beach sloping gently into the water, using Clearwater's pebble texture.
沢渡りSawatariStepping stones across the western water to the turtle island.
徽軫灯籠Kotoji-tōrōA two-legged lantern with one leg in the water and one on a rock, after the famous lantern at Kenroku-en.
雪見・春日灯籠Yukimi & Kasuga-dōrōSnow-viewing lanterns with a broad umbrella cap, and tall pedestal lanterns with hexagonal fire boxes.
三尊石Sanzon-sekiA triad of standing stones on the main hill.
庭木・大刈込Niwaki & ō-karikomiBlack pines pruned into cloud pads, often leaning over the water, and azaleas clipped into rounded mounds that flower in spring.
紅葉・桜Momiji & sakuraJapanese maples, which turn crimson and orange in autumn and are bare in winter, and cherries, which blossom in spring.
杜若・睡蓮Kakitsubata & suirenIrises at the shallow eastern margin and water lilies in the coves. Both flower in summer.
借景Shakkei“Borrowed scenery”: the blue ranges beyond the trees are part of the composition.
錦鯉NishikigoiFourteen koi in eight classic varieties.

Real gardens versus invention

The elements, their names and their proportions follow real practice (Kenroku-en's kotoji lantern, Heian fishing pavilions, Edo stroll gardens such as Kōraku-en and Ritsurin). The layout itself, with the pond's shape and the positions of every tree, rock and lantern, is invented for this build. It was checked from a top-down plan before any 3D work.

Clearwater

The water is built on Clearwater, “real-time, photoreal shallow water in a single HTML file”, made by Aurélien at Lumaris: https://github.com/Aureliengmz/clearwater. Clearwater's own references are Jerry Tessendorf (Simulating Ocean Water, FFT waves), Evan Wallace (WebGL Water, refracted-grid caustics), Inigo Quilez (texture repetition, used for the seabed tiling) and Marc Olano & Dan Baker (LEAN Mapping, distant highlights).

This build

The garden generation, the rendering around Clearwater, the koi, the seasons and the interface were written for this page. Techniques used: surface-gradient bump mapping (Mikkelsen), a Kasten–Young air mass for sun colour, Henyey–Greenstein in-scattering (as in Clearwater), and planar reflections with slope distortion.
